Map key & channel rules

Red square P = port mark. Green triangle S = starboard mark. Yellow N/E/S/W = cardinal: pass on the named compass side. Yellow × = special purpose, not a channel edge. Black/red ! = isolated danger. Grey ? = other or unclassified aid. V denotes a virtual AIS mark with no physical buoy. Symbols are simplified.

Marks are not always paired. Do not aim at a marker or assume a straight line between markers is safe. Keep to the right side of a narrow channel where safe and practicable. Check official charts for depths, hazards, speed zones and direction of buoyage.

What the labels mean

BoatMate's own conservative comfort estimates—not an official sea-state scale. “Glassy possible” means very light forecast wind, not guaranteed glass. River labels use wind and gusts only; no river wave forecast is available.

Wind bands (knots): glassy possible below 4 with gusts below 6; calm below 7 / 10; light chop below 11 / 16; choppy below 16 / 22; rough below 22 / 28; very rough above those. Both wind and gust limits must be met.

Coastal labels also use significant wave height, taking the rougher estimate: below 0.2 m, below 0.4 m, below 0.7 m, below 1.2 m, below 2 m, then very rough. Low wind with swell is described as “Swell / rolling” rather than glass. Missing coastal wave data prevents a comfort rating. Short-period waves can raise the estimate further.

Thunderstorm and poor-visibility forecasts override the comfort label. The calmest window compares two consecutive daylight hours; it is not a safety assessment. Times are Perth time (AWST). Hourly forecasts cannot predict the exact minute conditions change. Forecast downloads run hourly on GitHub; scheduled updates can be delayed. Data older than three hours is not displayed.
